It is one of the first questions people ask us. Sugaring and waxing both remove hair from the root, and both leave your skin smooth for weeks. So are they the same thing? Not quite. The differences look small on paper, but many people notice them on their skin.

Here is an honest comparison, including the times when waxing may suit you better.

## What they are made of

Sugar paste is made from sugar, water and lemon. That is the whole recipe. It is thick and golden, and it dissolves in water.

Wax is usually made from resins, often with oils, beeswax or other added ingredients. There are many kinds, from soft strip wax to hard wax that sets on the skin. The exact mix depends on the brand.

Neither is right for everyone. If you have an allergy or a sensitivity to any ingredient, including lemon, tell us before your treatment.

## Temperature

Sugar paste is used warm, at around body temperature. It feels a little like warm honey on the skin.

Wax is usually heated much more, so that it spreads thinly. A good therapist checks the temperature carefully, but hot wax on sensitive skin is one reason some people look for an alternative.

## Direction of removal

This is the biggest practical difference.

With sugaring, the paste is worked into the skin against the direction of hair growth, then flicked off in the direction the hair grows. The hair slides out the way it came.

With strip waxing, the wax is usually spread in the direction of growth and pulled off against it. Pulling against the growth can make some hairs more likely to snap rather than come out whole. Broken hairs grow back sooner, and they can be more likely to become ingrown.

## What sticks to what

Wax sticks to the hair and to the top layer of the skin. That is one reason skin can look red or feel tender afterwards, especially if the same patch is waxed twice.

Sugar paste grips the hair rather than the skin. Many people with sensitive skin find it easier to tolerate for this reason. And because the paste washes off with water, there is no need for oil-based removers to clear sticky residue.

## Comfort

Both methods sting for a moment as the hair comes out. Anyone who has tried either will know that feeling.

Many people who have tried both find sugaring more comfortable. The paste is warm rather than hot, and it removes hair in the direction it grows. How it feels still depends on the area, your skin and whether it is your first visit. We cover this in detail in [Does sugaring hurt? An honest answer](/does-sugaring-hurt/).

## Results

Because both methods remove the hair from the root, the results are similar. They usually last three to five weeks, depending on how fast your hair grows. With regular visits, many people find their hair grows back finer.

Both work best on hair about 5 mm long, roughly the length of a grain of rice. Our guide on [how long hair should be for sugaring](/how-long-should-hair-be-for-sugaring/) explains the timing.

## When waxing may suit you better

Sugaring is not the only good option. Waxing may be the better choice for you if:

- **You already wax and your skin is happy.** If it works for you and you trust your therapist, there is no need to change.
- **You want to do it at home.** Home wax strips are easy to find and use. Sugaring by hand takes a lot of practice to get right.
- **Sugaring is not easy for you to get to.** Waxing is offered in far more places.

## How laser is different

Laser works in a different way altogether. Sugaring and waxing take out the hair you have now. Laser targets the pigment in the hair to reduce regrowth over time.

## So which should you choose?

If you have sensitive skin, have had a bad experience with waxing, or simply like a short list of ingredients, sugaring is worth trying. If waxing works well for you, there is no pressure to switch.
