Small red bumps after hair removal are frustrating. They can itch, look sore and take a while to settle. If you get ingrown hairs often, you are not alone. They are one of the most common reasons people come to us.

Sugaring may help reduce ingrown hairs for some people, but no method of hair removal can stop them altogether. Here is what causes them, how sugaring may help, and what you can do at home.

## What is an ingrown hair?

An ingrown hair is a hair that grows back into the skin instead of up and out of it. It might curl round under the surface, or grow sideways before it breaks through.

Your skin treats the trapped hair as something that should not be there, and reacts to it. That is why you see a small raised bump. It may be red, and sometimes you can see the hair curled underneath.

## What causes them

A few things make ingrown hairs more likely:

- **Shaving.** A razor cuts the hair at an angle and leaves a sharp tip, which can pierce the skin as it grows back.
- **Hairs that break.** If a hair snaps below the surface instead of coming out whole, the new tip may not find its way out.
- **Dead skin.** A build-up of dead skin cells can block the opening of the follicle and trap the hair.
- **Curly or coarse hair.** Hair that naturally curls is more likely to bend back into the skin.
- **Friction.** Tight clothing, such as skinny jeans or tight underwear, can press hairs flat as they grow.

They tend to show up most on the bikini area, underarms and legs, and for men on the beard, neck, chest and back.

## How sugaring may help

Sugaring removes hair from the root, in the direction it grows. Because the hair slides out the way it came, it may be less likely to break under the skin. Fewer broken hairs can mean fewer sharp tips growing back the wrong way.

There are a few other reasons many clients notice a difference:

- The paste grips the hair rather than the skin, so the skin is often less irritated afterwards.
- With regular visits every four to six weeks, many people find the hair grows back finer.
- You stop shaving between appointments, which takes away one of the most common causes.

Results vary from person to person. Some clients see a clear change within a few visits. Others still get the odd ingrown hair, especially if their hair is very curly. We would rather be honest with you than promise something no one can.

## Looking after your skin at home

What you do between appointments matters as much as the treatment itself.

- **Give your skin 48 hours.** Avoid hot baths, saunas, pools, the gym and tanning for two days after sugaring.
- **Wear loose cotton.** Especially after bikini sugaring, loose cotton underwear and loose clothes reduce friction.
- **Exfoliate gently, from a few days later.** A soft mitt or a mild scrub two or three times a week helps clear dead skin so new hairs can come through. Do not scrub hard, and do not start in the first couple of days.
- **Moisturise.** Soft skin makes it easier for new hairs to grow out. A plain, fragrance-free moisturiser is a good choice.
- **Use SPF.** Freshly treated skin can be more sensitive to the sun.
- **Try not to shave between appointments.** If you do need to, just tell us at your next visit.
- **Do not pick or squeeze.** Digging at a hair with tweezers or nails can break the skin and leave a mark. If a hair is sitting just under the surface, gentle exfoliation often helps it come through on its own.

## When to see a pharmacist or GP

Most ingrown hairs settle by themselves within a few days. Speak to a pharmacist or your GP if a bump:

- is very painful, hot or swollen
- is filled with pus or keeps getting bigger
- has redness that is spreading, or you feel unwell or feverish
- keeps coming back in the same place, or is leaving dark marks or scars

A pharmacist can often suggest a suitable treatment, and will tell you if you need to see your GP.
